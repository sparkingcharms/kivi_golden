# User Interviews

## User Interview Questions

I asked users these 5 questions, with some questions slightly rephrased depending on the conversation:

1. **How do you currently use voice on your phone or computer, and what do you usually use it for?**
2. **Tell me about the last time you wished you could do something on your computer just by speaking. What were you trying to do?**
3. **When you use voice typing or AI voice tools, what frustrates you or makes you stop using them?**
4. **Do you ever mix languages while speaking, like Tamil + English or Hindi + English? How well do your current tools handle that?**
5. **If you could tell your computer what you want in natural language instead of clicking through menus or typing, what would you want it to do for you?**

## Insights Gained from all Interviews

### 1. Understanding the context matters more than just getting the words right

A common frustration I heard was that AI can pick up most of the words but still understand the request differently from what the person meant. People want the AI to understand their actual intention and edit based on their context, not based on what the AI thinks sounds better.

### 2. Voice becomes much more useful when it knows what I am looking at

One person brought up a very practical example of working with hardware and soldering, where they might be looking at a PCB schematic and want to tell the laptop what to do without stopping their work. This made it clear that voice should work together with the screen and the task the person is already doing.

### 3. People want the AI to learn how they work

The people I spoke to wanted more personalisation and customisation. They don't want to keep explaining the same preferences every time. They want the AI to gradually learn their vocabulary, the way they communicate and the way they like things to be done.

### 4. People expect the AI to do more than answer questions

One of the stronger responses was the idea of having something like JARVIS. The expectation is that the AI should be able to remember things, bring information back when needed and eventually do useful tasks. Examples that came up included organising folders, retrieving files and changing display or system settings.

### 5. There is a desire for an AI that is always there on the laptop

I got the feeling that people don't just want another app they open for a specific task. They want something that is always available while they are studying, coding or working, almost like a friendly assistant they can talk to whenever they need help. We already see more of this feeling on phones, but there is still a gap on laptops.

### 6. Multilingual interaction is a real need, not just a feature

While talking to someone in the mess, live translation came up as a useful need. People also naturally mix languages when they speak, especially in India. So multilingual understanding may not be the first reason someone starts using a product, but it can make the experience much more natural once they do.

### 7. Voice should help with communication, not just typing

In everyday conversations, including the way people use WhatsApp, the need is often bigger than simply converting speech into text. People want to say something once and then change the tone, language or format depending on who they are talking to. The important part is keeping their meaning while changing how it is expressed.

## Overall insight

After these conversations, I feel the opportunity is much bigger than voice typing. People want an AI that understands what they mean, understands what they are working on, learns how they work, remembers useful things and can eventually help them get things done.

The bigger opportunity seems to be:

> **Voice + Screen Context + Personalisation + Memory + Action**

And ideally, it should feel less like using an AI tool and more like having a helpful AI companion on your laptop.
