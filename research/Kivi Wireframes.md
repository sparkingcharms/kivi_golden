# Kivi Wireframes Reference

The complete Kivi wireframes are maintained in the main Kivi product repository as **Kivi wireframes.pdf**. This reference records the interaction model that the Golden Goose implementation should remain consistent with.

## Core interaction

**Point → Speak → Understand → Preview → Apply**

The app underneath stays where it is. Kivi arrives on the relevant selection, takes the instruction, makes the interpretation visible, shows a preview where a change is proposed, and leaves the final action to the user.

## Product surfaces represented in the wireframes

- **Kivi Anywhere**: the floating voice layer available across the computer.
- **Green Selection**: the selected content becomes the context for Kivi.
- **Understand**: explanations stay beside the content being read.
- **Write**: selected text can be changed by voice, with a preview before applying.
- **Think**: messy spoken thoughts can become structured decisions, questions and tasks.
- **Communicate**: one intention can be expressed differently for different audiences.
- **Write · code**: developers can describe a change to selected code and review it before applying.
- **Remember**: useful things can be remembered and brought back later.
- **Teaching Kivi**: the full Kivi space brings together Styles, Dictionary and Memory.

## Five intentions

**Think** — organise what I am thinking.

**Write** — create or change what I want to say.

**Understand** — make sense of what I am receiving.

**Communicate** — express what I mean appropriately to someone else.

**Remember** — learn how I work.

## Visual and interaction principles

- Kivi is a layer over the user's existing workflow, not a destination.
- The screen provides context and voice provides the instruction.
- Kivi should feel lightweight when resting and more present when invoked.
- Important interpretations and proposed changes should be visible.
- Changes should be previewed before they are applied.
- The user remains responsible for the final action.
- Multilingual and code-mixed speech is part of the interaction, not a separate mode.
